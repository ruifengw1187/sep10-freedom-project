Introduction
Animation is the art of making static images look like they are moving. You draw a lot of them, each slightly different, and play them at 12 to 24 frames per second so your brain blends them together. There are many types, like 2D, 3D, stop-motion, and motion graphics. Animators use the "12 Principles of Animation" to make movements look good. Animation has come a long way, but the goal is still the same: making fluid animation.🥶

Part A: Existing Technology

Software
When I searched for "Software Animation Tools," I found a website called [MoonB] (Link here). It listed the 13 best AI animation tools. One tool is King AI, which makes realistic animation and consistent characters for cheap ($10/month). It has good motion physics. Another tool is [Luma Dream Machine] (Link here), which turns still images into cinematic animation. It makes smooth 5-second shots really fast.

I also found [Adobe Animate] (Link here). It has a cool feature where you use your webcam and mic to capture live movements. It's great for making vector graphics for cartoons and games. Another software I found on [10 Animation Software Platforms] (Link here) is Cinema 4D. It's used for animating, modeling, texturing, and lighting.

Hardware
When I searched for "Hardware Animation Tools," I found a site called [6 types of equipment] (Link here). One tool is a stylus pen. It's better than paper because you don't have to deal with eraser crumbs, and it's just more convenient. A dedicated drawing tablet or iPad is also super useful. I also found a [System Requirements Guide] (Link here). It says a strong GPU (like an RTX 4070) is crucial for complex scenes. You need at least 16 GB of RAM (32 is better for 3D), a good CPU for rendering, and at least 1 TB of storage because animation files are huge.

Part B: Possible Future Technology
For future tech, I found an article called [The Future of Animation: What to Expect in the Next 5 Years] (Link here). It talks about how AI is making animating easier. Instead of drawing every single frame, you can just type a prompt, which saves animators a ton of time. It also talks about VR, AR, and MR being used in animations where your choices can change the plot.
